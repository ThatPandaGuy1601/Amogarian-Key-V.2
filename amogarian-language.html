GlyphsMeaning

Grammar in brief

  • Plural: add -ai. ixel (star) becomes ixelai (stars).
  • Adjectives follow the noun and never change: ixel nolum, "silent star."
  • Prepositions come before the noun: ruvath zarun, "over the world."
  • Verbs take a person prefix: ne- I, du- you, ka- we, so- they. So kavael is "we watch."
  • Tense: add -uth for the past and -ora for the future: kavaeluth (we watched), kavaelora (we will watch).
  • Negation: put ol before the verb: ol kavael, "we do not watch."
  • Numbers come before the noun: thavor ixelai, "five stars."
  • Articles: none. zarun means "world," "a world," or "the world," depending on context.

Key: letters

The script has 26 letters, one for each letter of the English alphabet, plus two single glyphs for th and sh. Every glyph follows a few rules, so a reader who learns them can guess unfamiliar ones.

How the script works

Vowels are round. Every vowel is built from a circle or a curve. The stroke that leaves the circle (up, down, or through it) tells you which vowel it is. W, the half-vowel, is drawn from curves too: a double cup, like the letter's name.
Consonants are cut. Every consonant is a straight spine with angled branches, and none of them contain a circle. Only x drops the spine.
A dot above means voiced. Six consonants come in pairs, and the voiced one is the same shape with a dot on top: k/g, t/d, p/b, s/z, f/v, c/j. Here c is the ch sound and j is its voiced partner.
Two sounds get their own glyph. th and sh are single glyphs, not two. Typing ch gives the single glyph c.
Direction. On a line, the script runs left to right. On a ring, such as the crest, it runs clockwise from the top, with the tops of the glyphs pointing outward. The glyphs are spaced evenly all the way around, so three glyphs sit at 0, 120 and 240 degrees.
Numbers are base five, written in dots (see the Key: numbers tab).

Reading rules

These rules generate the small readings above the glyphs. They were built so that the pronunciations already in the lore come out exactly as written there (see the table below).

  • Syllables. Every vowel makes its own syllable. A consonant between two vowels joins the vowel after it, except n and m, which close the vowel before them (ban-isk, am-oh-gah-ree). When two or more consonants meet, they split in the middle, but s before t, k or p stays together (nik-star).
  • Open and closed. A vowel at the end of a syllable is long: a = ah, i = ee, o = oh. A vowel followed by a consonant in its syllable is short: i = ih, and o = o as in "on," except before r, where it stays oh (as in "more"). The vowels e, u, y never change (eh, oo, ih).
  • The vowel a also shifts by what follows it inside a syllable: before l it becomes eh (val is "vell"), before n or m it becomes a as in "cat" (ban), and before anything else it stays ah (tar is "tahr").
  • x is always the two sounds k + s, and the two can fall in different syllables (nik-star).
  • Doubled consonants are one strong sound. The second glyph is shown in brackets, like (r).
  • Helper vowel. If a word ends in three or more consonant sounds, a quick ih slips in before the third (Ralixnth = rah-LIHK-sihnth). It is shown dimmed above the glyph it belongs to.
  • Stress falls on the first syllable, except in words that end in a helper syllable, which stress the syllable before it.

Key: numbers

Counting is in base five, and the digits are made of dots. Five is therefore written "10," the way we write ten. That makes five the "round number" of the language, which suits a species that named its mission after five stars.

The five digits

How place value works

  • Columns are worth 1, 5, 25, 125, and so on, with the biggest on the left.
  • Once you reach five you roll over, the way we do at ten. So 5 is "10" (one five, no ones), 7 is "12," and 25 is "100."
  • To convert a number, divide by 5 repeatedly and keep the remainders. For 12: 12 ÷ 5 = 2 remainder 2, so it is written 22.
  • Reading aloud: digits are said one at a time, so "10" is ren-oru. The word thavor ("five") is the spoken name for that number.

Operatives

The five current members of the DaRainbow Initiative, the original Blue, and the Japanese defector. Each codename is also written in Amogarian, spelled by sound. [CNG] marks what the lore withholds.

What the five have in common

All five are Inter-Galactical Diplomats (IGDs) from Valrion, sent to Earth on the ASTRION for the Silent Constellation mission. On Earth they took fabricated human identities and were noticed by Churchill and Roosevelt, who organized them into the DRI. Each wears a RAVEN suit in their own colour. After the war they stayed four more years, then went home, and they have returned every ten years since to check on Earth.

The original five were Arthur, Peter, Simon, Charles and Sean. When Arthur was lost, his son Carl took his place, which gives the current five: Carl, Peter, Simon, Charles and Sean.
